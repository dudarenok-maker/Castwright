## #2643 negative control — attempted by emulation, empirically CLOSED as not producible here

Method (`a33-launch-neg.ps1`, `a33-neg.ps1`, `a33-loadneg.ps1`, `a33-warm.ps1`): sidecar with **no device pins** and `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=''` in the process environment — the closest reachable shape of "a box with no CUDA build/device at all" on hardware whose two cards are CUDA-capable.

Result: the mask did **not** produce the required CPU-only state — measured, not assumed.

- A real unpinned `POST /synthesize` (kokoro, `kokoro-v1.0`, 44 chars) went through end-to-end: cold `gen_ms=64975 rtf=24.37` (ORT CUDA graph build), then a **warm re-run `gen_ms=491 rtf=0.18`** producing 128000 bytes of real PCM (`a33-evidence/a33-warm-synth.wav`) — unambiguously GPU-speed numbers.
- `/health` inside that same masked process (`a33-health-N2-loadneg.json`, `a33-health-N3-warm.json`): `cuda_verified: true`, `devices.kokoro: "cuda"`, `vram_reserved_mb_by_device` enumerating both cards, no `stale_reason`.

On this sidecar's ORT build, CUDA provider enumeration and session construction are not suppressed by a per-process device mask; with physical drivers up there is no way to instantiate the negative control's precondition ("auto-resolution's own intent is `cpu`"). The 2026-09-06 "permanently untestable on this hardware" conclusion is therefore confirmed by direct measurement — and the obvious escape hatch (process-level masking) is now closed by evidence rather than merely untried. No defect filed: this is standard ORT/NVML behaviour, recorded as a methodology finding per this row's own protocol.
