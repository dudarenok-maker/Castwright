## Run C — attempted `reverted`-branch demonstration (structurally unreachable here)

Goal: observe auto-revert *rewrite a pin* on hardware. Setup: qwen pinned to
card 0 (`tts.qwen.device: cuda-uuid:GPU-1831b67f-…` in the scratch settings),
eager Qwen+Kokoro preload, `SIDECAR_VRAM_FREE_FLOOR_MB=6500`. Card 0's free
fell to 5016 MB under the loaded qwen 0.6B — below the floor — yet **no
self-exit followed**, because the floor breach only fires on *non-torch-active*
cards and the pinned engine's own CUDA context makes its card active the moment
it loads there.

That is the general law on this box, and it kills every route to `reverted`
on hardware:

1. **Floor breach ⇒ tripped card is torch-inactive ⇒ nothing is resident
   there ⇒ `planForCard` returns `unrevertable`** (Run B's exact outcome).
2. **Reserved-ceiling breach (torch ≥ threshold) ⇒ the card IS active, but any
   threshold low enough to breach while qwen sits on card 0 (~1.85 GB
   reserved) is also breached by the same footprint wherever the revert moves
   it** — both cards are far larger than every engine here, so it would
   ping-pong the pin 0→1→0 indefinitely rather than settle. A `reverted`
   demo that immediately trips again proves the mechanism's decision, not the
   revert's stability — and manufacturing a stable revert requires a card too
   small for the model, which this machine does not have (that is precisely
   the scenario the 2 GB-card design targeted).

So the `reverted` branch on *this* hardware is not inducible without
arbitrary local modification of the trip logic — recorded as a limitation,
not faked. Its decision logic remains covered by the paired tests + two
mutation runs documented in `step-3-a3-build.md` and the #3148 chain.
