# Castwright 1.15.0

**What's new in 1.15.0**

- **Clone a voice you have permission to use.** Record a short sample, confirm it's yours or a family member's with their say-so, and cast it in any book, on both speech engines, in any language, though a clone sounds most like its owner in the language it was recorded in.
- **A voice library that follows your series.** Design a voice once in My voices and cast it anywhere. "Don't reuse" gives a character a voice of its own, and redesigning a shared voice retires its old emotion takes in every book that shares it.
- **Analyse and describe your cast with your own model server.** Point Castwright at llama.cpp, LM Studio, vLLM, llama-swap or OpenRouter, set how hard each analyzer thinks, and pass your own settings to the model.
- **Dialogue recognised in more books.** More quote-mark styles in all seven languages (Swiss «…» only partly), steadier speaker-matching in dash-punctuated books such as Russian, and a book's language read from its chapters instead of its copyright page.
- **Characters keep their identity through a re-analysis.** Re-rendering a chapter now puts the right voice back (chapters affected before this update need a fresh re-render), and you can tell Castwright that two names are the same person.
- **Busy books ask you to wait instead of breaking.** Renaming, restructuring or re-parsing a book while it renders, analyses or exports is refused with a plain message, and a second simultaneous cast change waits, giving up with a message after about ten seconds.
- **Your graphics card, better looked after.** Voice designs and Quality previews free the room they need on an 8 GB card, and a speech engine that hits a memory ceiling or a transient GPU error restarts on its own.
- **Sturdier settings and paired devices.** A damaged settings file no longer stops Castwright starting, and a paired device with a damaged expiry date is turned away instead of kept forever.

- **An emotion take can now be designed for a voice the analyzer gave no description.** Designing a take from the character drawer failed with "No persona to design from", and "Design full cast" either failed the character or wrote a new description that the main voice was never designed from, even though the main voice was already designed. Both now use the description the main voice was designed from. The Voices page also used to fail for every book while one book's cast file was unreadable; it now skips that book and shows the rest.
- **Designing a voice, previewing at Quality, or starting a Quality chapter render on an 8 GB graphics card now just works after a render.** Castwright unloads the standard voice model, and Kokoro if a render used it, before it needs the room, and tells you when it does for a design or a Quality preview. It never unloads anything while a chapter is rendering or another voice design is running; if a design or preview is refused during a render, the message says to wait for the render to finish, or to pause it. And it no longer tells you to turn off "Preload Kokoro" when that setting is already off.
- **A voice design no longer gives up just because a chapter repair was running when you pressed it.** Castwright now waits for the repair to end and frees the room then, instead of spending its one try too early and failing. Nothing changes while a chapter render is running: Castwright still never unloads anything under a render. If the design is still refused, the message says a chapter repair is in progress and to wait for it to finish, instead of telling you to pause something that cannot be paused.
- **Rebaselining a series from the Voices page no longer writes into a book it did not change.** Redesigning a character's voice for a book other than the open one used to copy that book's persona and voice into the same character of the open book, and the next save stored it there. Now a rebaseline updates the loaded book's voice only when the server actually changed that book (the book itself, or a linked character in it), and otherwise leaves it as it was.
- **Choosing "Don't reuse" now gives the character a voice of its own straight away, so redesigning it never touches the earlier book.** Before, redesigning a character you had set to "Don't reuse" also rewrote the earlier book's voice and its emotion variants. Now the character moves onto its own copy of the voice the moment you choose it, and the earlier book keeps its voice exactly as it was. Your choice sticks: analysing the book again no longer links the character back. If saving fails, Castwright tells you and leaves the link as it was. If another book in the series can't be opened when you choose it, Castwright asks you to try again and changes nothing. Characters you declined before this update behave as before.
- **"Don't reuse" now fully renames the character's voice to its own.** The character already played its own copy of the voice; the name stored with it still pointed at the earlier book's, which could confuse anything that reads the name. It now points at the new voice, in every book that moved with it.
- **Designing a voice, or an emotion take, for a character you chose "Don't reuse" on is no longer refused because of a clone in the earlier book.** The earlier book's cloned voice is not the one the character moves onto, so Castwright now designs it. It still refuses when a clone sits on the character's own voice or on another character that moved onto the same new voice, and it does so before saving anything: a refused design leaves the voice and its emotion takes as they were; a character the design first had to move onto a voice of its own stays moved.
- **"Don't reuse" waits until every book in the series can be read.** If one of them couldn't be opened, the books linked through it kept the earlier book's voice, and redesigning one could change it. Now Castwright asks you to try again and changes nothing; the same goes for redesigning a character you chose "Don't reuse" on before this update. Books an earlier "Don't reuse" already left behind can only be moved through the API (repeating the request); the app doesn't offer it again.
- **Linking a character back to a book you chose "Don't reuse" on now gives it that book's voice again.** Before, the link came back but the character kept playing the voice it had designed since. Characters in the same book that shared its voice, and books that follow it, come along too.
- **Designing a voice before you confirm a book's cast no longer changes earlier books.** A suggested match can be wrong, so until you confirm the cast, a design or emotion take stays with that character, which first gets its own copy of the voice it shared. Confirming doesn't change that: the character keeps the voice it designed. To give it the earlier book's voice again, link it to that book by hand. Characters you design after confirming share as before.
- **Designing the same character in two books at once now gives both books one voice.** Before, running Design full cast on two books of a series together could split the character across two voices.
- **Designing a character's voice in a later book no longer breaks its emotion takes in the earlier one.** When a character from an older book shared its voice with a later book, designing it there gave the earlier book's voice a new name but left the angry, sad and other emotion takes behind, so those lines failed to render. The takes now go with the voice to its new name, so they keep playing while you compare a new design, and still play if you cancel it. Once you keep a new voice, they are retired along with the old one (see the next note). The same goes for a character that is not in a series.
- **An emotion take that finishes only after the other book's voice has been moved over now keeps playing too.** Before, a take whose design completed after the series had already moved onto the shared voice was answered as done but recorded nowhere, so its lines spoke the base voice. Now the take lands on every book that was moved onto the shared voice, with its files copied to the new name. A book that chose "Don't reuse", or that has a voice of its own or a newer design of the shared voice, keeps what it has; and a character whose voice moved onto a new name now shows that new name.
- **An emotion take you design in one book while the same character is being designed in another book of the series now keeps playing.** Before, a take that landed at that moment could be left behind under the old name, or replaced by the version it had just superseded, so its lines failed to render or spoke the old take. Now the take goes to the new name as it stands when each book moves over, and a take already designed under the new name is never overwritten. If that move fails partway, no stray copies of the voice are left behind.
- **Redesigning a shared voice now retires its old emotion takes in every book that shares it.** The angry, sad and other takes were designed against the old voice, so keeping them left those lines sounding like a different character. When you approve a new base voice, or design one straight over the existing voice, every book sharing that voice reads those lines in the new voice until you design the takes again, and the book you have open drops the old takes straight away, with no reload. A book where you chose "Don't reuse" has a voice of its own and keeps its takes; one that declined before this update still shares the voice, so its old takes are retired too.
- **Removing an emotion from one book's character no longer takes it away from another book.** If the same character in another book of the series still uses that emotion (because it shares the voice, or chose "Don't reuse" before Castwright gave it a voice of its own), its lines in that emotion keep playing. The same goes for approving a compared voice, which clears the character's emotions: the other book keeps its own. Removing an emotion now only takes it off that character; the emotion's voice file stays until the next time Castwright starts, which then clears away every emotion voice no character in your library uses any more.
- **Two books that ended up sharing one ID are now sorted out for you.** Castwright notices on startup, lets the one with your work keep it, gives the other its own, and tells you which folders were involved. The copy keeps its exports and analyses, and an analysis of the copy that was already done or half done carries over. If a browser tab was left open while Castwright restarted and repaired a pair, that tab reloads itself once, the first time it tries to save a change to a book's content, so it never saves over the wrong book; a change you were typing at that moment is not kept. A voice change or Design full cast refused in such a stale tab is repeated after the reload, and then applies to every book. A tab left open from an older version can't reload itself and quietly refuses to save, so reload any Castwright tab you left open before upgrading. Listening progress and shelf changes are never held back.
- **Reordering the render queue while two chapters are rendering no longer drops one of them.** If you dragged a chapter to a new place just as the queue held two renders in progress, Castwright kept only the first and the other quietly disappeared; now every chapter that is rendering stays where it is.
- **When two chapters start rendering at the same moment, both now show as Rendering.** Before, one of them could keep showing Queued while it was actually being narrated, because the two updates to the queue overwrote each other; they now apply one after the other.
- **A character split into two entries in a new analysis no longer takes a series link you refused.** If you had told Castwright that a character in this book is not the one from an earlier book, a re-analysis that happened to list that character twice under one name could still hand them the earlier book's voice link and lose your refusal. Your refusal now holds when the analysis lists that character under two new ids.
- **"Preview as listener" is gone, and with it a crash.** The button on the Listen page took the whole app down when you pressed it, so we removed it. Everything else on the page works as before.
- **A book whose chapter titles changed under a parser update can be edited again without a re-parse, and a book whose chapters were reordered is caught before you analyse it again.** Merging, splitting, reordering and renaming chapters no longer refuse when the parser names a chapter differently, as long as the chapters still hold the sentences you analysed. If a book's chapters have been shuffled out of place, Castwright says so when you start analysis, instead of attributing the wrong text to the wrong chapter; Start fresh still works. Importing over a book or restoring a backup that changes its chapters means analysing it again before you generate, while an ordinary restore of the same chapters keeps your speaker corrections. Restoring a backup is turned away, with a note, while something else is running on that book, and removing empty chapters from a book whose chapters don't line up is refused.
- **A book saved while your library loads no longer shows a stale copy.** If Castwright read a book's saved state in the instant it was being replaced, it could fall back to the previous backup and warn that the file was missing. It now waits for the save to finish and reads the new version, and your settings file is read the same way.
- **Opening a duplicate review twice on the Voices page no longer leaves a hidden timer running.** If you opened it again while the other book's voices were still loading, the second request kept checking every fraction of a second for as long as the page stayed open, and never noticed the voices had arrived. It now shares the first request and stops.
- **Moving or renaming a chapter no longer throws away a take you were still comparing.** If you had re-recorded a chapter and not yet chosen between the old and new take, reordering, renaming or refreshing chapter titles used to drop that choice. Castwright now keeps both takes with the chapter, so the comparison is still waiting for you. Merging or splitting the chapter still clears it, because its audio changes, and a chapter that takes over another's place never picks up an old take that wasn't its own.
- **Reordering chapters no longer gives a chapter another chapter's audio.** If you had excluded a chapter and then restored a backup, Castwright could lose track that the excluded chapter still had finished audio. Moving chapters around afterwards left that audio behind, and the chapter that took its place played it. Castwright now remembers the audio of excluded chapters too, so it moves with its chapter.
- **Excluded chapters no longer count toward runtime or turn up in Regenerate.** The Generate view's runtime, the character Regenerate estimate and the stale-audio notice now ignore chapters you have excluded, Regenerate for the whole book starts from the first chapter that is in the book, and the drift report no longer offers to regenerate an excluded chapter. If you exclude a chapter from the Generate view while its preview is waiting, the preview is cancelled and you are told.
- **Deleting a book and importing it again from its bundle no longer brings the deleted book's cast back.** Before, reopening the re-imported book could show the old cast and save it over the one in the bundle.
- **Voices matched automatically on the Confirm screen no longer vanish a moment after they appear.** Before, opening the screen could re-read the saved cast just as the matches were applied and show the older, voiceless cast again.
- **A change you make just before leaving a book is no longer lost if saving it hiccups, and a retried save can't overwrite something newer.** If Castwright can't save your last edit as you go back to the Library, it tries again a few times, and if it still can't it tells you instead of quietly putting the old version back. Merging, splitting or reordering chapters waits for that save, and if it still can't be saved, stops and asks you to try again. If the book changed in the meantime, in another tab or from the Library, Castwright tells you the earlier edit wasn't saved and reloads the book rather than letting an old edit overwrite a newer one. Editing a book's title from the Library and reopening it now shows the new title.
- **Saving a book's details no longer snaps an earlier change back if a later one fails.** If you saved a new title and then, while it was still saving, a second change to the book's details couldn't be saved, Castwright put the title back to what it was before both, even though the first save had gone through. It now goes back only as far as your last change that saved, and a detail it has already saved is no longer sent a second time alongside a later change, so a change made somewhere else in the meantime isn't overwritten.
- **A book detail you just saved no longer looks like it snaps back when the book reloads at the same moment.** If you saved a new title or other detail just as the book was being re-read, the old value could flash back on screen until the next reload. It now stays as you saved it.
- **Refresh titles on a book whose chapters you reordered or merged now keeps each chapter's own title.** After you moved chapters around, the Refresh titles action in the chapter restructure view could hand a chapter the name of whichever chapter used to sit in its place, so a chapter Castwright had named "Chapter 2" could come back as "Prologue". Castwright now remembers which part of your manuscript each chapter came from, so reordered, excluded and merged chapters keep the right names, and a merged chapter keeps the title you gave it. Books you reshaped before this update behave as before.
- **You can now tell each analyzer how hard to think.** Thinking models spend part of their answer budget reasoning first, and on a long chapter that can leave nothing for the answer. Advanced settings now has a reasoning control for each Ollama and Gemini model and every OpenAI-compatible endpoint, offering only the levels that model or server can take. Nothing changes until you pick something. The Test button checks which levels a model accepts, and a refused level stops a run before it starts. One honest caveat, shown next to the setting: a server can accept a level and quietly ignore it, and Castwright can't see that. If a model spends its whole answer budget thinking and writes nothing, Castwright says so and stops.
- **A custom parameter can no longer switch off the structured output your OpenAI-compatible server is asked for.** Castwright already refused the request parts it depends on; it now also refuses the llama.cpp, vLLM and LiteLLM spellings that could strip or replace them, such as vLLM's structured-output fields and LiteLLM's drop and fallback settings. `enable_thinking` is refused unless the endpoint's Reasoning control is set to Not controllable. You get a message naming the key, and a setting saved earlier that includes one is quietly left out of requests. To have a LiteLLM proxy drop unsupported parameters or fall back to another model, set that in the proxy itself.
- **Listen back-to-back now works.** When Fix audio, or a voice-change preview, gives you a new take of a chapter to compare, one tap plays the old take and then the new one, start to finish, each from the top. Stop whenever you like. Acting on the comparison ends it cleanly instead of starting the next take: pressing Reject draft or Commit selection (in a voice-change preview, Reject & re-adjust or Approve — regenerate the rest), or a take's own play button. A take that has no audio, or is being re-rendered, is skipped, and the button waits, and says so, while the takes are still loading.
- **Following a link to a setting in Advanced settings no longer risks leaving the row lit.** If your settings reloaded in the two seconds after you arrived, the highlight could stay on; it now always fades.
- **The analyzer endpoint editor is easier to tap on a tablet or phone.** Its text boxes and the context-size box now have full-size touch targets; on a computer with a mouse they look the same as before.
- **Pass your own settings straight to the model.** Want `top_k`, `min_p` or a presence penalty on your local server, or `topK` on Gemini? Each analyzer engine and every OpenAI-compatible endpoint now takes a small block of custom parameters that Castwright adds to every request. It won't let you override the parts it depends on, such as the model name, the output format, reasoning and the context size (on Gemini, anything outside a short list of generation settings), and tells you exactly which keys it refused. A temperature you set there shapes the first try; a retry uses Castwright's own. The run label shows "+ custom params" whenever they're in play, and your values never appear in logs. Keys and passwords don't belong there: each has its own field.
- **Merging, splitting, reordering or renaming chapters on a book whose chapter list is out of date now stops and asks you to re-parse, instead of losing a chapter's audio.** If a book's chapters no longer matched its manuscript, a merge or split could delete a chapter's finished audio and shuffle its lines onto another chapter. Castwright now checks first, changes nothing, and tells you to re-parse the book from its menu in the Library (which clears its analysis, cast and audio).
- **Analysing a book whose chapter list is out of date now stops and asks you to re-parse, instead of scrambling it.** Running or re-running analysis on a book whose number of chapters no longer matched its manuscript, because it was merged earlier or because Castwright now splits its text into a different number of chapters, could bring back chapters you had merged, put a chapter's identity on the wrong one and leave its finished audio behind. Castwright now checks first, changes nothing, and tells you to re-parse the book from its menu in the Library (which clears its analysis, cast and audio, so a book you have not rendered yet loses its cast).
- **Importing a book you already have no longer ties the two copies together.** When you import a book bundle for a book Castwright already has, and it keeps both, reshaping the chapters of one used to disturb the other. Each copy is now its own book, so a copy imported on the same device opens fresh, the way one from another device does, rather than borrowing the original's analysis.
- **Switching books no longer risks saving one book's cast, script or history into another.** An edit made in the split second while a book was still opening could land in the wrong book, and opening a book with nothing saved yet could keep showing the previous one. Castwright now keeps each book's edits with that book, and a brand-new book starts clean. Unsaved changes to a book's details are cleared when you open a different book, so they can't end up on the wrong one; come back to the same book and they're still there.
- **You can write your cast's voice descriptions with your own model server.** If you've connected an OpenAI-compatible server such as llama.cpp, LM Studio or vLLM for analysis, you can now pick one of its models under Persona generation engine in Advanced configuration. It shares the same limits as analysis. If the server is missing, Castwright tells you before it asks for anything and never quietly switches to Gemini. If audio is being made on the same graphics card, Castwright asks whether to pause it first; Escape means Wait, and if the queue can't be paused, the design doesn't start and you're told. A model on that same card that stops answering ends Design full cast with one message; any other model reports its failure for each character it couldn't write. Half-finished thinking is no longer saved as a voice description.
- **Analyse with your own OpenAI-compatible server.** Point Castwright at llama.cpp, llama-swap, LM Studio, vLLM or OpenRouter, tell it which graphics card the server uses, and pick its models anywhere you choose an analysis model. Castwright asks before analysing on the card your voices are using, frees that card when a voice model needs it, and shows whether the model really follows its output format. If a server can't be reached, analysis falls back to Gemini by default (when you have a Gemini key); switch that off, or send it to your local model or another server, in Advanced Settings. Once an analysis has fallen back, it stays on the fallback for that analysis; the next one tries your server again. A new wiki page walks through setting up each server.
- **Renaming or deleting a book while it's rendering, analysing, designing voices, reviewing or exporting now asks you to wait, instead of quietly splitting it across two folders.** Renaming a book while something else was still working on it could leave a stray copy of the old folder behind and the book's cast out of step with its new name. Castwright now takes turns: quick edits finish first, and for a long job it tells you the book is busy and to try again when it's done. A rename that can't go through because a chapter is playing or open in another program now says so, too, instead of showing a raw error.
- **Including a chapter that hasn't been analysed yet no longer makes it vanish on the next restructure.** Merging, reordering or excluding other chapters used to quietly delete a chapter you had included but not analysed, text and all, and renumber the ones after it. Castwright now keeps every chapter that has text and only drops one with nothing in it, like a lone heading.
- **Pinning a standalone book's character to a voice tier now saves.** On a book that isn't part of a series, choosing a tier for a character did nothing; it now sticks.
- **After you change a character's voice, the voice check now compares new takes with the new voice.** Re-rendered lines used to be flagged as the wrong voice, and a character with too few lines to check could stay unchecked for good. The check now measures against the character's current voice only; chapters still in the old voice count as inconclusive on the QA card rather than flagged, and the voice-drift notice on the Revisions tab points you to re-render them. Renaming, splitting or merging chapters no longer drops a chapter's voice-check results, and reordering no longer leaves audio behind under old names, and fixing one line of a character's dialogue on the Kokoro voice no longer takes that character off the voice check.
- **Renaming a book while its manuscript is being re-parsed now waits for the re-parse, instead of risking a half-reset book.** Re-parsing or replacing a manuscript clears the book's cast, audio and analysis before it rebuilds the chapters, and renaming the book at that moment could move its folder out from under that work. Castwright now holds the rename until the re-parse finishes, then moves the finished book.
- **Re-parsing or replacing a book's manuscript while it's rendering, analysing, scoring, designing voices, reviewing a script or exporting now asks you to wait, instead of wiping the work in progress.** Both of these rebuild the book's chapters from its manuscript, which clears the book's cast, audio and analysis first — and doing that while a render or export was still writing those files could quietly throw away the work in progress. Castwright now tells you the book is busy and to stop the job or wait for it to finish, the same way it already does for renaming or deleting a busy book.
- **Changing chapters while a book is busy now asks you to wait.** Merging, splitting, reordering, renaming, refreshing titles or excluding chapters while a book is rendering, analysing, scoring, designing voices, reviewing a script or exporting is now refused, because a render that finished mid-shuffle could file its audio under the wrong chapter or bring back audio you had just excluded. Castwright tells you the book is busy and to stop the job or wait, including when you click Exclude in the Generate view, where Include is also unavailable and the row says what is running. Cancelling an include takes the chapter back out; if the book stays busy and that can't be done, Castwright tells you the chapter is still included.
- **Two chapters rendering at the same time no longer trip over each other.** When two chapters of one book finished together, one could occasionally fail with a file error, or leave the other's finished-chapter details unsaved. Castwright now takes turns when it records a finished (or failed) chapter, so both are kept.
- **Restructuring a book no longer renames a chapter that really is called "Chapter 1".** Merging, reordering or excluding chapters, and refreshing titles, used to renumber a heading taken from your manuscript — so in a book that opens with a Prologue, "Chapter 1" turned into "Chapter 2". Castwright now renumbers only the placeholder "Chapter N" titles it made up itself for chapters with no heading. Older books are recognised automatically when their chapter titles still match the import, and a title you type yourself is never renumbered.
- **Merging, splitting or reordering chapters now survives a restart.** If you reshaped a book's chapters and Castwright restarted before you analysed it again, the analysis could undo a merge or read a chapter's text under another chapter's title. Castwright now saves the reshaped chapters with the book, picks them back up after a restart, and keeps them when you export and re-import the book. A book whose chapters you reshaped before this update isn't repaired: if its chapters read wrong, re-parse it and reshape them again, and from then on they're kept.
- **See every analyzer model you can use, set its limits, and test it.** Advanced Settings now lists the models your Ollama install, your Gemini key and your OpenAI-compatible servers actually offer, lets you set per-model request limits, and has a **Test** button that checks whether a model really follows Castwright's output format before you spend a whole book on it — including thinking models, and without mistaking a too-small context for a missing feature.
- **Reparsing or replacing a book is now refused, before anything is touched, if its review history was saved by a newer version of Castwright.** This is a safeguard for the future: rather than discarding that history along with the old cast and audio, Castwright asks you to update it first and leaves the book exactly as it was.
- **Restarting Castwright no longer rolls an m4a or ogg chapter back to its earlier take.** If you were holding a new take for review on a book rendered as m4a or ogg, the startup check mistook your earlier take for a leftover and swapped it back in over the new one on every restart. It now leaves a take that's awaiting your decision alone.
- **Resuming an analysis no longer occasionally redoes work it had already saved.** In rare cases two progress saves landed in the wrong order, so the older one overwrote the newer. Analysis progress and the manuscript's chapter text are now saved in the order they happened.
- **A Gemini model that thinks before it answers no longer stalls on a long chapter.** Some Gemini models reason before replying, and that reasoning counted against a fixed reply limit, so on a big chapter the model could spend its whole allowance thinking and hand back nothing. Castwright now lets each Gemini model reply up to its own limit, keeps showing activity while the model thinks, and waits up to two minutes for a reply to start instead of giving up after 45 seconds. When a model genuinely runs out of room while reasoning, Castwright stops starting new work and says so plainly, with what to try next and a link to the right row in Advanced Settings. A local model that fills its context window before writing any answer no longer reports an empty reply: Castwright splits the chapter, or tells you the model ran out of room while thinking. If you already set your own Gemini output limit, Castwright keeps it.
- **A model that "thinks out loud" before answering no longer trips up analysis.** Some models open their reply with a private reasoning note before the actual answer. Castwright used to stumble over that note, try the chapter again, and often give up on it. It now sets the note aside and reads the answer that follows.

- **Phase cards now show when an analysis is paused, halted, or needs input, so you can see at a glance why nothing's happening.** When you pause an analysis or it hits an error and stops, the phase cards stay visible but shift to show that state. When a run pauses because some chapters failed cast detection, attribution coverage came back incomplete, or the cast would shrink by more than half, the cards show a neutral "needs input" state, the top-bar Status pill shows "Needs action", and the library card shows "Needs input — review?". Resuming flips the cards back to active. Pausing a book in one tab no longer makes a different book look paused in another, a pause from another device no longer leaves a "streaming" bar above cards that say paused, and a healthy analysis is no longer declared "halted" just because a background connection closed.

- **A damaged settings file no longer stops Castwright from starting.** If `user-settings.json` can't be read, Castwright restores it from the most recent of the three backup copies it keeps of every save. If none can be read, it carries on with factory defaults and shows a banner on every screen until you next save a setting, saying what happened. The damaged file is kept aside as `user-settings.json.corrupt-<timestamp>` on that next save, so nothing is lost. Repairs you make to the file outside the app are picked up automatically and never overwritten by stale values.

- **The analysing screen no longer looks busy before you've told it to start.** Opening the analysing view used to show a spinner, a progress bar and a "streaming" label right away, which once led a reader to wait on a run that hadn't begun. The screen now stays quiet until you click Start, and a run you reload, resume or return to mid-way still shows as in progress. A run that failed in its very first moments used to hang instead of reporting the failure; it now reports it right away.

- **Designing a single character's voice could get permanently stuck if it hit trouble the moment it started, and that locked out the whole book.** If Castwright ran into a problem reading the cast list as a voice design began, the design hung forever and the book stayed marked as mid-design, so analysis and every other voice design were refused until a restart. Such a design now fails cleanly right away and releases the book.

- **Startup and update failures now say what broke, instead of only that something did.** The server log now names the part of Castwright that failed when a speech engine won't start, a settings file can't be read, or the record of an update can't be written. If only the record of a successful update failed to save, Castwright used to skip the restart that should have followed; it now restarts anyway. A failed script-review start now logs its real cause, shows a plain message instead of one that could expose a file path, and closes its open connections cleanly. An update can still get stuck showing "applying" with no automatic way out; that is unchanged, but the log now says why. None of this recovers failures automatically; it only makes them visible.

- **A chapter you'd just confirmed past the loud-fallback prompt could sit waiting in the queue instead of picking straight back up.** When Castwright pauses a chapter to ask whether to render an undesigned voice through its fallback engine, clicking "Render anyway" is supposed to send it straight back to work. It sometimes didn't — the chapter could sit queued for a while with nothing else happening until some unrelated action nudged things along. Castwright now lets go of that chapter the moment it pauses, so confirming it re-starts the render right away.
- **"Render anyway" on that same fallback prompt now actually renders.** Even after the fix above, confirming the prompt could still leave the chapter exactly where it started. The confirmation itself was never reaching Castwright's server, so the chapter was quietly put back on hold every single time you clicked it. It now reaches the server, and the chapter renders.
- **A chapter that loses its connection partway through no longer stalls your whole queue.** If Castwright's server became unreachable, restarted, or ran out of attempts to reconnect before a chapter finished, that chapter used to sit forever at "Generating" or "Stalled" — holding onto one of your render slots the whole time. With the default two slots, two such chapters in a row could freeze the entire queue until you reloaded the page. A chapter like that now shows as Failed with a Retry button, and the rest of your queue keeps moving.
- **A cast full of voices is designed together now, even when one of them can't be.** "Design full cast" used to stop dead the moment a single voice couldn't be designed — a missing API key, say — leaving the rest of the cast untouched and the reason hidden behind a generic failure. Now, one voice's failure doesn't hold up any of the others: the run keeps going, and the summary you're left with names the actual reason that one voice didn't come through. Starting a second design while one is already under way, or a second full-cast run while another is mid-flight, now tells you plainly that a design is already running, instead of quietly doing nothing.

- **A character whose name Castwright already knew in plain letters can have that name put back.** In some books analysed before a recent fix, Castwright swapped a character's internal name from its plain-letters form to a Cyrillic one, which could leave a narrated chapter unsure whose line it was reading. This release adds a repair pass for books it already happened to. It only acts when the book's own earlier snapshots prove both names belong to the same character, never touches a book with nothing to fix, refuses to run while Castwright might be open, and keeps a dated copy of the files it changes, so there is always something to go back to.

- **A character whose name is spelled a little differently in your cast and your manuscript now shows up correctly everywhere Castwright checks their voice.** Some characters were missing from Revisions' drift warnings, the Voices screen's designed and generated counts, and the optional speaker-quality check. Chapters rendered before this fix need a re-render to pick it up. When you change a character's voice and re-record only some of their lines, Revisions keeps flagging the chapter until every line is in the new voice (for chapters rendered with this version or later; an older chapter may go quiet sooner), the Voices screen keeps counting the old voice as generated until then, and a "Fallback (Kokoro)" label clears once you've re-recorded every fallback line with Fix audio.

- **Books in a language Castwright can narrate with its second voice engine now actually generate.** If your book was not in English and any character still needed a voice, Castwright refused the chapter and told you to design the missing voices, even when its other engine could narrate that language. Castwright now only refuses when it has no way to voice the language, and otherwise lets you carry on as it does for English.

- **On a two-graphics-card machine, a mid-chapter voice repair now asks to happen on the card that isn't already busy narrating.** A voice that needed rebuilding during generation used to land on whichever card it fit, possibly the one already narrating, risking a stumble in your chapter. It's a request, not a rule: if the other card is too busy or absent, the repair falls back to ordinary placement, and your own `tts.coqui.device` pin, if set, still wins.

- **Designing a voice no longer competes with narration for your graphics card, and a design that gives up can no longer take the card down with it.** Castwright pauses Kokoro while a voice design runs, but the pause used to lift a moment too early, so the two could end up on an 8 GB card together. The pause now lasts the whole design, so a chapter rendering on a Kokoro voice waits while a design runs; during a bulk "Design full cast" run that wait can cover most of the run, and it is deliberate. When two designs overlap, narration waits for the last one, not the first. A design that gave up waiting for a busy model used to leave a lock behind and every later design failed until a restart; it now always hands the lock back.

- **Analysing a book no longer warns you that someone else changed your cast when nobody did.** Castwright warns when you edit a character while an analysis runs, since the analysis could overwrite the edit. But it didn't count its own start-up tidying of merged characters as its own work, so it could report a conflict that never happened. The warning is now reserved for the real thing.

- **A short line of dialogue in Russian, French or Spanish can no longer land in the wrong place in your chapter.** When a line like "да." ends up identical to a snippet inside an earlier word — Russian "да" hides inside "правда" (truth), "вода" (water), and "когда" (when) — Castwright used to occasionally grab the wrong occurrence. A dash marks where each line actually starts in these languages, and Castwright now uses those markers to anchor short replies in place, fixing the most common cases where they used to land with the wrong speaker.

- **The speech engine now recovers from transient GPU trouble automatically on every platform.** When the speech engine hits a memory ceiling on your GPU and recycles itself, or a transient CUDA error poisons it, Castwright now restarts and recovers on Windows, Linux and macOS alike, including when you start the engine yourself with `npm run tts:sidecar`. It retries up to five times with growing pauses before giving up. A crash after the engine ran healthy for 30 seconds or more counts as a fresh incident, so isolated crashes hours apart never add up to a permanent failure.

- **The startup log now tells you when the speech engine's runtime changes.** If Castwright detects that your GPU runtime has been replaced with the standard CPU version — or has gone missing entirely — it now says so plainly in the logs at startup, instead of silently taking the change in stride. That visibility helps you spot a misconfiguration before it affects a generation.

- **A character whose name spells a little differently between your manuscript and your cast now shows their real line count, and Fix audio can find them.** If a character's dialogue was attributed under a slightly different spelling than their cast entry — a hyphen where the cast used an underscore, say — their line count in the Generate view could come up short, and the Fix audio tool couldn't find the chapters they appear in. Castwright now recognises both spellings belong to the same character everywhere it matters, so their line count is correct and Re-record is reachable for every chapter they're in.

- **Retrying a different chapter while a subset retry is already running now shows you what's happening instead of silently joining the wrong run.** If you click Re-analyse on chapter 5 while chapter 3 is already retrying, Castwright tells you on the row that chapter 3 is in progress, and the running retry stays on screen so you can wait for it or cancel it and try again. Resuming a main analysis while that retry runs is also blocked now, so two jobs can't overwrite each other's progress.

- **On Windows, updating Castwright now reliably picks up speech-engine fixes instead of sometimes deciding there's nothing to install.** On some Windows setups the check for whether your voice engine needs reinstalling was thrown off by how Windows saves text files, so an update could conclude nothing had changed. Pinokio updates get the same fix, so an old Pinokio install stops triggering a spurious reinstall on every update. One consequence: your next update, through the Pinokio Update button or Account → Application updates, will run a one-time reinstall of a small piece of the voice engine. It is a longer update than usual, once, and then back to normal.

- **CI's weekly quarantine check now catches when it can't read the register.** The automation that tracks which tests are known to be flaky used to parse the register's format incorrectly and silently report a false clean — now it either reads successfully or reports the failure instead, so a broken parse won't hide the problem until someone notices weeks later.

- **Advanced Settings now explains what each chunk-size setting actually controls**, instead of leaving you to guess how it interacts with the others.

- **Castwright's on-box acceptance register checker now catches stale content markers on test rows.** The internal tool that verifies acceptance-test artifacts against what's already published used to ignore row titles and risk badges, only checking the row body. A row whose test status changed from "20 of 60 run" to "10 of 60 run" would ship without a trace, invisible until someone glanced at the live page. Now it catches changes to either, so a test row's published state stays in sync with what you're about to ship.

- **The acceptance-register citation checker now catches when a register row has been reorganised but code is still citing it by its old id.** Castwright keeps detailed internal records (the acceptance register) of on-box test results, deployments, and known gaps — every entry tagged by a unique id. When a review or reorganisation shifts entries around, citations in the code can end up pointing at the wrong row, silently. Castwright now detects that mismatch, with an exemption for reorganisations that were already documented as intentional. A false-alarm in multi-row entries — when one row of a subject was archived but others stayed — is also fixed.

- **The register citation checker now also catches when a citation's heading text has drifted from the row it points to.** When a row's title in the acceptance register changes and the citation in the code hasn't been updated to match, Castwright spots the drift and notes it — this one's advisory only, since title wording can legitimately vary, so it's a heads-up rather than a hard stop.

- **Running Castwright's test suite while several worktrees are busy no longer risks a multi-hour pileup.** A test run now notices when another copy of the codebase is already testing and slows down, the way it already did for a busy graphics card, so parallel runs stop crashing each other. Asking for only the tests a change touches now selects them from the change itself.

- **Saving your work is instant again, and the checks moved to where they belong.** Every commit used to run a large slice of Castwright's test suite on your own machine first, which could wedge for hours when several copies of the codebase were working at once. A commit now checks the code style of just the files you staged, and a push runs its safety guards plus, only when you have touched the speech engine, the one test suite the cloud can't run. Everything else is checked in the cloud, where it blocks a change from shipping. Nothing is trusted that used to be verified; it just happens somewhere that doesn't stop you working.

- **You can't restructure a book's chapters while it's being analysed.** Merging, splitting, reordering or excluding chapters could be silently undone when an analysis finished a moment later. Now the Restructure button waits, with a note saying why, until the analysis is done or paused — and while a book is being restructured, Start, Resume and Retry on its analysis are switched off with a note saying why (and if a start still slips through, you are asked to try again in a moment, not shown a failed analysis).

- **Takes that finish while you're on another book now wait for you as normal A/B reviews.** A preview that finishes while you're elsewhere opens when you come back, and closing one no longer loses it: reopen it from the Status pill. Takes stuck on "Rendering…" from before the update become reviewable again, marked "Recovered from before the update," when the earlier take is still on disk. Restructuring a book (merge, split, reorder, exclude) no longer lets another chapter pick up a take it wasn't recorded for, and merging keeps every other chapter's audio (the merged chapter itself has to be generated again). Its edit controls are disabled with a note saying why while it runs. Not everything is protected yet: a manuscript save built before a restructure can still replace the manuscript file once it lands, and a chapter finishing generation can still write to the book while it restructures. A few edge cases remain, such as a sentence merged away from mid-chapter coming back after a reorder.

- **A broken on-box acceptance register can no longer slip onto main.** Castwright tracks detailed test results and known gaps in an internal acceptance register, and the register itself has safety checks that catch when it's been damaged or become inconsistent. Those checks used to run but weren't wired as a required gate, so a bad register could ship to main anyway. Now those checks block a PR from merging, keeping a broken register out of the codebase.

- **GPU speech synthesis works correctly again on NVIDIA boxes.** The speech-engine library was pinned to a version newer than the rest of Castwright's stack is built for, causing it to silently fall back to your CPU instead of using your GPU. Every sentence ran slower with no warning that your graphics card wasn't being used. The library versions are now aligned, so your GPU does the work again and gives you audio at GPU speed. When Castwright needs to repair your GPU venv, it now shows you the repair command in both PowerShell and standard shell forms, so you can run it straight from Windows — it was previously written for POSIX only.

- **The third reason your GPU could sit idle during speech generation is now closed too.** Even with the GPU runtime and its supporting library installed, Kokoro still ran on your CPU, because the speech library's own check for a GPU could never say yes on any machine. Castwright now makes that decision itself and hands the engine a ready-made GPU session. As with the previous fix, the last word belongs to real hardware, so it stays flagged for on-hardware confirmation.

- **A separate, quieter reason your GPU could sit unused during speech generation is being worked on.** Castwright's speech-engine setup installed the GPU runtime but skipped a small supporting library it needs to run on your graphics card. Nothing failed or warned you; Kokoro just ran on your CPU. The library is now installed alongside it, and the startup log says plainly whether it actually loaded. Confirming your GPU is really used still needs verification on real hardware, so consider this one in progress rather than done.

- **The alarm meant to catch a silent CPU fallback couldn't actually ring.** Castwright is meant to flag when Kokoro asks for your graphics card and lands on your processor, but on every install that never touched the advanced device setting, the alarm compared against a setting nobody had set and could never fire. It now compares against what that moment actually asked for, so a genuine silent fallback is caught, while a CPU placement Castwright chose on your behalf to manage memory stays quiet.

- **That same alarm now watches the moment that actually matters.** On an ordinary book generation nothing explicitly asks Kokoro for anything, so a slip to your processor still read as "nothing to report". Castwright now works out before each generation whether your graphics card was genuinely the plan, so a silent slip on an ordinary render trips the alarm too. A machine with no usable GPU is unaffected.

- **Kokoro now tells you plainly when it falls back to your processor instead of your GPU.** All the fixes above make sure your GPU actually gets used — but even with a perfect setup, if something goes wrong, Kokoro might still slip onto your CPU without a word. An amber warning now appears on the device panel when that happens, describing the GPU runtime problem. A CPU-based placement Castwright chose on purpose to manage memory stays quiet — it's only the silent fallbacks that get flagged.

- **And that's the last of it: Kokoro genuinely runs on your GPU now, on a fresh NVIDIA install.** Every fix above closed one way the fallback could happen without ever proving the underlying problem was gone — a fresh install was still missing two small pieces of the GPU runtime, and a separate part of that same runtime was pinned to a version that didn't actually match what the rest of Castwright's speech engine needs, which could silently break other voices as a side effect of fixing this one. Both are now correct, and this time it's been proven, not just reasoned about: a fresh install, a real generation, real GPU usage measured while it ran.

- **Loading Kokoro during a voice design no longer risks interrupting the design.** On a machine where voice design and Kokoro share one graphics card (the default setup), clicking the Load model button on the Status panel while a voice design was running could interrupt the design with no warning if both ended up on the same card. Castwright now ensures the load waits until any active design has finished and released the card, protecting the design's real-time interactivity the same way a background chapter render already was.

- **Installing Qwen3-TTS from inside Castwright no longer fails with "Access is denied", and no longer quietly costs you your GPU.** On Windows, Admin → Model Manager → Installed models → Install Qwen3-TTS failed every time the app was running, and showed pip's routine "new version available" notice instead of the real error. Castwright now pauses the voice engine for the install (queued chapters simply wait), runs the installer, checks that the graphics-card build of the runtime is still in place, reinstalling it if the installer swapped in the slower processor-only build, and starts the engine again. It refuses to start if a chapter is mid-render or if the engine wasn't started by Castwright. If bringing the engine back doesn't work, Castwright points you at Restart sidecar. A real failure now shows its real reason, with a note on whether your GPU runtime came through intact.

- **The same fix now applies to Coqui XTTS, Kokoro, and Whisper, with no more "Access is denied" on any of the three installer buttons.** Each pauses the voice engine so queued chapters wait, refuses to start if a chapter is actively rendering, checks afterwards that the GPU runtime is still in place, and shows the real failure message instead of a generic pip notice.

- **Changing a character's voice no longer drags their old sound into every new line.** Castwright's behind-the-scenes check of how each character's voice is holding up compares their audio against a stored reference of that voice. Reassign to a different voice, and that reference used to keep describing the old one — so accurate, freshly recorded lines were flagged as mismatches and re-recorded against a speaker they were never meant to match. The reference now remembers which voice it was built from and rebuilds itself the moment you change the voice, so a character finally sounds like who you actually cast.

- **Re-analysing a book no longer creates a duplicate cast member when a character's name picks up or drops a surname between passes.** Castwright matches your characters between analyses by looking at their names, and a small variation — like a surname token appearing or disappearing — used to look like a completely new person. Your existing cast member kept their voice and all previous recordings; the fresh "duplicate" landed with nothing recorded yet, forcing you to re-record. Now Castwright tolerates small changes like that so the same character is recognised across analyses and your cast stays intact.

- **A character's private id no longer flips to a wrong alphabet during re-analysis.** When re-analysis minted near-duplicate candidates for the same person, Castwright's cleanup could record a working ASCII id tag as retired in favour of a freshly minted non-ASCII one. The character's voice still rendered correctly, but it broke the repair tools for id drift in older books. Castwright now recognises that collision and keeps the established tag.

- **A background render can no longer pile onto your voice design mid-load.** While you're designing a voice in the cast review, Castwright may start recording a chapter in the background — and if a render arrived while the design's model was still loading, Castwright used to go ahead and load a second model at the same time rather than wait, risking running out of graphics-card memory for both. The design is shorter and you're watching it render in real-time, so it takes priority: a background render now waits for the design's model to finish loading before it takes its turn.
- **A render no longer fails outright just because you're designing a different character's voice at the same time.** Once a voice design is up and running, Castwright previously gave any other render trying to use the same graphics-card memory very little time before giving up and reporting there was no room — even though the design would genuinely free that memory again soon. A render caught behind an active design now waits substantially longer before giving up, on the understanding that the design is about to finish and hand the memory back — so switching between cast review and generation no longer means the occasional spurious failure.

- **✨ The headlines.** Voice cloning arrives, on both speech engines — record a short sample you have permission to use and cast it like any other voice, in any language, though a clone sounds most like its owner in the language it was recorded in. A character keeps their identity through a re-analysis, so a narrated chapter no longer quietly hands their lines to the narrator (a chapter affected before this update needs a fresh re-render). Castwright frees graphics-card memory by itself for a voice design or a Quality preview, rather than telling you there is no room. And re-recording one character can no longer put their voice on somebody else's lines. Everything below, in more detail.
- **The clone wizard now tells you, before you record or upload, which language to record in.** A voice cloned from a sample in one language sounds markedly less like you when it speaks another — cloning a voice from a Russian sample and having it speak Russian keeps far more of the original voice than an English sample speaking that same Russian line does. The wizard now says so up front, while you're choosing what to record.
- **If you assign a cloned voice to a book in another language, Castwright says so at the time, not after you have waited for a chapter.** It names the character, the language the voice was cloned in and the language of the book, and tells you the clone will sound less like the person it came from. It does not stop you: the voice is assigned anyway and the choice stays yours. If Castwright doesn't know your book's language, it says nothing rather than guessing.
- **Castwright now works out what language your book is in by reading the book, not its copyright page.** The language used to be decided from the opening pages, so a German novel behind a short English foreword was read as English and every voice and pronunciation was wrong from then on. Castwright now looks at your actual chapters, skips the front matter it recognises, and weighs each chapter's language by how much of the book it covers. It needs a decent amount of text before it trusts an answer, so a short book can come back undecided instead of guessed at. Books you've already imported keep the language they have.
- **Your cast list now keeps your book's own spelling of every name.** Castwright never actually asked for names back in the book's own language, so a Russian novel could come back with "Anton Gorodovsky" and "Boris Ignatievich" instead of Антон Городецкий and Борис Игнатьевич. Those names also help Castwright tell your characters apart as it works through chapters. It now asks for names exactly as your book spells them.
- **A book imported outside the normal screens no longer gets quietly treated as English.** Castwright works out your book's language when you add it and shows you the answer on the confirm screen. A book added another way, such as by a script, used to lose that answer and be assumed English, which decides the voices you're offered, how names are pronounced and how dialogue is recognised. Castwright now keeps the language it detected unless you tell it otherwise, and what you choose on the confirm screen still wins.
- **A book that never stated a language — not even in that detection — is no longer silently treated as English when it could matter.** Anything that forces a choice for a book whose language was never set — re-recording or repairing one of its chapters, designing a voice, generating audio, reviewing its script, or offering you voices to use — used to quietly behave as if the book were English, and there was nothing on screen to say otherwise. Castwright now stops and tells you that no language is set for that book, instead of guessing English at any of the points where that guess would steer the result.
- **And now there's actually a place to fix it.** A book with no language set now shows plainly on your library card instead of wearing a language badge it never earned, and Book settings gains a language field. Whatever stopped you, whether designing a voice, starting analysis, reviewing a script or generating audio, opens that same field as a prompt so you fix it on the spot and carry on. If Castwright can't tell your book's language at import, it says so and lets you decide now or later, rather than pre-picking one.
- **A book that comes back with everybody's dialogue read by the narrator is now caught while it's being made, not after you listen.** Castwright checked that nothing was dropped or repeated but never whether your cast had actually been used, so a Russian novel with nearly every spoken line handed to the narrator passed every check. For books that mark speech with a dash (Russian, French and Spanish), Castwright now notices when spoken lines are going to the narrator, has another go at that stretch, and tells you if it still can't get it right. A dash-led aside like «— said Yegor» is narration and never counts against it. Books that mark speech with quotation marks are untouched.
- **That narrator-catch above got sharper.** When Castwright has another go at a collapsed stretch of a chapter, it now keeps whichever attempt is genuinely less collapsed rather than settling for whichever one happens to look tidier by an unrelated measure. It also now notices when a model drops the dash that marks a line as spoken in the first place — not only when it hands that line to the narrator outright. And a chapter that still can't be fixed now tells you plainly that the problem is your cast, instead of showing the unrelated "some sentences went missing" message it used to show for this exact failure.
- **Pressing Retry on one of those collapsed chapters now actually checks whether it worked.** A chapter Castwright had flagged for handing every line to the narrator used to come back marked fixed the moment a Retry finished — even on the rare occasion the fresh attempt collapsed exactly the same way. Retry now checks its own result the same way the original analysis would, so a chapter that's still ignoring your cast stays flagged instead of quietly looking resolved.
- **A settings file saved by an ordinary Windows text editor no longer resets every one of your preferences.** Castwright keeps your settings in a small file you can open and edit yourself. Save that file from Notepad — or from a PowerShell script — and Windows quietly adds three invisible bytes to the front of it. Castwright couldn't read the file at all after that, and rather than telling you, it fell back to its factory settings for everything: your analyzer, your engine, your sidecar preference, all silently back to the defaults you'd changed. It now reads the file regardless of those bytes, so an edit you make by hand sticks.
- **The time estimate on a long analysis no longer claims to be measured when it's a guess.** While Castwright works through your book, it shows an estimate for the attribution stage — and it used to say that figure came from timing the earlier stage on your own machine. It never had: the number shown is a fixed guess calibrated on entirely different hardware, and it said so in a way that read like a measurement. Two separate runs of the same book would print the same estimate to the second, which no real measurement does. It now says plainly that it's a pre-flight guess, refined only afterward — not a claim of having already timed your machine.
- **A Russian book no longer re-reads a whole chapter because of the word “as”.** Castwright double-checks that it hasn't missed anybody who speaks in your book, by looking for names sitting next to words like “said”. In Russian, the ordinary phrase “As the mage said…” puts the word *как* — “as” — in exactly that position, so Castwright decided *как* was a character it had somehow overlooked, and re-read the entire chapter from scratch to look for them. On a full-length novel that's a quarter of an hour of work for a word that means “as”. It now knows the difference.
- **Castwright now spots when your analyzer's model is split across multiple GPUs, which slows things down.** When an Ollama model is divided between more than one graphics card, performance drops sharply and nothing used to warn you. Castwright now detects the split and logs a warning. You can declare which GPU you've pinned Ollama to with the "Expected analyzer GPU" setting in the Advanced Configuration screen's "Analyzer models & endpoints" section, so the warning can say what got misplaced. It is informational only; Castwright never changes your Ollama placement on its own.
- **Advanced Settings no longer shows an "Analyzer engine" control that did nothing.** That choice moved to the Model Manager as a per-account setting, but the old control lingered, inert. It's gone, and the analyzer device display now updates when you change your choice in Model Manager. Three messages that still sent you to the old place are corrected: the "Qwen3-TTS isn't installed" nudge links to the Model Manager, the mixed-voice-engines warning names the real "Keep both voice engines loaded" switch, and the hint for re-authorizing this browser points at Admin → LAN access.
- **Clone a voice you have permission to use — and cast it anywhere.** Record or upload a short sample, confirm it's yours (or a family member's, with their say-so), and Castwright builds a reusable voice you can hand to any character.
- **Build your own stable of narrators in My voices.** Design a voice once — from a persona, with a live audition — and it's yours to cast in any book, not just the one you designed it for. Not happy with a take? Redesign it and compare old against new side by side before you commit. Fallen for a voice you built for one character? Save it to My voices and hand it to someone else next time, in a different book entirely.
- **A cloned voice tells you clearly when it can't be used — instead of quietly rendering as someone else.** If Castwright can't rebuild a cloned voice as itself, it stops and says so, right down to the reason — a revoked voice, a missing sample, or a book set to the wrong engine — so you always know a chapter used the real voice or didn't run at all. That promise now holds on **both** of Castwright's speech engines, not just one: until now a cloned voice only worked reliably on one of them, and casting it on a book that used the other could either hit a wall or, worse, quietly get you a stand-in.
- **A cloned voice now reads a non-English book in that book's language.** Cast a cloned voice into a German or Russian book and every line came out as unintelligible English-sounding speech. Your clone is language-neutral, and now the book decides, so the same clone reads whatever you cast it into. Clones you made before this update still work, though My voices keeps labelling them English until you re-record or rebuild them. New clones are labelled with the language you spoke.
- **Fixing a misheard word in your voice sample now actually counts.** When you record a sample for cloning, Castwright writes down what it heard — and if it misheard you, you can correct it. Until now those corrections were quietly ignored, and the clone was built against the wrong words. Your edit is now what the voice is built from, and it sticks, so a later touch-up doesn't undo it.
- **Cloning a voice for a child now records who actually gave consent — not the child.** Cloning a family member's or a child's voice used to write the consent record as if the person being cloned had attested to it themselves, which got the guardian relationship exactly backwards. The clone wizard now asks who's attesting whenever it isn't the person themselves, and that's the name that gets recorded — and a clone on someone else's behalf can no longer be recorded without it at all. If the name is missing, Castwright refuses the clone rather than quietly filing the consent under the wrong person.
- **Revoking a cloned voice now really does erase it.** Your original recording, and everything Castwright built from it, is gone for good — not just the visible entry. Castwright tells you exactly what you're about to lose and asks you to confirm before it happens. Revoke also holds up under an unusual spelling of a voice's own key: Castwright now refuses to play a voice under anything but its canonical name, closing a corner where a copy could keep working after a revoke reported everything erased.
- **Your consent record for a cloned voice is now enforced on every path that could carry it.** That check now reaches the last couple of paths inside your own install that hadn't been covered: linking one character's voice to another's, and a plain cast save. A cast save that simply leaves a cloned character's voice out no longer silently drops it — it's restored — and one that tries to swap it for something else is refused, and refused as a plain refusal rather than a crash. A voice you've revoked, or never gave consent for in the first place, can't be quietly carried across any of them.
- **Casting a voice across a whole series now respects a clone anywhere in it, not just the book you're looking at.** Carrying a voice assignment to the same character on every linked book used to check only the book being written, so a clone on another linked book could be silently retargeted or muted. Castwright now checks the whole series first, and refuses the change outright if it would have touched nothing but cloned characters. If the change would apply to some linked books and not others, it still applies everywhere it safely can; telling you which book it skipped is not yet in this release. Your cast screen also flags a character as protected the moment a linked book carries a clone for them.
- **A cloned voice can no longer be silently overwritten by a redesign.** Editing a cloned voice's persona used to be able to replace it in place with a completely different, synthesised voice — while everything on screen kept calling it by the original name and nothing warned you. Redesigning or promoting a cloned voice is now refused outright, so the voice you cloned stays the one you get.
- **Designing your whole cast at once no longer knocks a cloned character off their clone.** A bulk "Design full cast" used to be able to retarget an already-cloned character onto a fresh, uncloned voice without telling you. It now skips them and names who, right in the summary — and designing a single already-cloned character on its own is refused with a reason instead of quietly doing the wrong thing.

- **Fixing a character's audio no longer makes the new take disappear before you've heard it.** After Fix audio or a line regenerated from the regen dialog, the new take stays in the Revisions section of the Status pill, where you can audition, accept or reject it, even after a reload or a trip to another book. If a book's revisions can't be loaded on the way back in, Castwright tells you and keeps trying. Two cases are still open: a take that finishes while you've left the book mid-batch can come back stuck on "rendering", and if saving a take fails just as you leave, coming back can erase it (#3421).
- **Stopping Castwright from the command line now reports what it actually did, and an in-app update restart no longer risks crashing silently.** On Windows, stopping could print `[GONE] … (already exited)` for an app it had just stopped; Castwright now checks the process is really gone first. Two pieces missing from a packaged release now ship: one the updater's restart depends on, and the one that keeps Windows awake during a long generation. So does the helper that sets up your secure local certificate: a packaged install with `mkcert` installed serves Castwright over `https://` on port 8443 instead of `http://` on 8080. Because that address is meant for your phone and tablet, the app now listens on all your network interfaces behind an access token Castwright mints for you; Windows may ask once to allow it through the firewall. Set `LAN_HTTPS=0` in `server/.env` to keep it on this machine only.
- **After you change a character's voice, "Re-record the flagged lines in this chapter" no longer judges a new take against the old voice for characters checked against their audition sample.** The check compared a new take against a reference captured at the last full render, so a voice changed since then was judged against the voice it replaced. For those characters the old reference is now dropped, and a line flagged only for not sounding like the character stays flagged for you. Characters with many lines are checked against a reference built from the book, which now follows their current voice. This only applies if you've turned on **Render-integrity QA (voice match)** and **Auto-fix voice mismatches** in Settings; both are off by default.
- **Two windows acting on the same new take can no longer cost you both takes.** If you committed a new take in one window while Reject was still working on it in another, the two could cross mid-way — one deleting the earlier take just as the other was about to put it back — and the chapter was left with no audio at all. Castwright now makes the two wait for each other, one chapter at a time, so whichever finishes second sees what the first one did instead of acting on a half-moved pair.
- **You can now choose how strictly the analyzer is told to answer in Castwright's format.** In Advanced Settings, the local analyzer and Gemini each have a "structured output" setting: "schema" spells out the exact shape of the answer, "json" only asks for valid JSON, and "off" asks for neither. Nothing changes unless you change it. When an analyzer turns a request down as invalid, Castwright now tells you which of your settings shaped that request, and key and access errors tell you which key to check and where it lives.

- **A failed Retry now stays failed, and a stopped analysis stops properly.** A chapter whose Retry fails stays marked failed, even after a reload, and a Retry no longer reports a book as finished while other chapters still need attribution; it asks you to resume the analysis instead. While an analysis is running, Retry, Re-analyse and Include wait for you to pause it, and a paused or halted analysis stops its model work straight away. Start fresh now un-confirms the cast, so the book shows as analysing until you confirm it again. Switching books while an analysis, voice-match or emotion detection is working no longer saves one book's cast, emotions or delivery directions into another. If emotion detection didn't finish, Castwright picks it up again quietly in the background the next time you open the book; books analysed before this release aren't picked up this way.
- **Re-analysing a book no longer makes your companion app download the whole book again.** Every chapter carries a stable identity that the companion uses to tell what it already has, and re-analysing used to drop it, so the next sync looked like an entirely new book. Re-analysis now keeps each chapter's identity. Two people importing a book with the same title at the same moment can also no longer overwrite one another: one import goes through and the other is told the book already exists, or both are kept under different names.
- **A book whose saved details were cut off mid-write no longer disappears from your library or blocks your edits.** If Castwright closed or the disk filled at the wrong moment, a book could show as unreadable or refuse every edit. Castwright now recovers from its most recent good backup and repairs the file on your next change. A momentary read hiccup from a cloud-sync folder no longer rolls back your last change or resets your settings to factory defaults. If your settings file is locked or unreadable, Castwright tells you, hides the settings form and holds off saving instead of showing defaults you could save over your real settings. Deleting a book whose details are unreadable, or one you uploaded but never analysed, now actually removes it from your library.

- **A cover can no longer break your m4b export.** An uploaded JPEG with extra data tucked on the end, or a GIF or WebP cover from a cover provider, could make the export fail. Castwright now saves every cover as a fresh JPEG, whether you upload it, it's downloaded for you, or it arrives inside an imported book bundle. The trade-off: an uploaded JPEG is re-compressed once, at quality 85, and loses its embedded metadata, though its rotation is applied first so it still sits the right way up.
- **Some server log lines now show the values they were meant to.** A handful of messages about voice deletion, voice style, queue and export problems printed a raw `%s` placeholder where the book or voice belonged, with the actual name tacked on at the end of the line. They now name the book, voice or file in place. Behind the scenes, an IP-address library used by the server is updated to its latest security release.
- **Revisiting Confirm no longer swaps out a voice you locked or tuned.** When Castwright matched a character to one from an earlier book, it could replace the voice you had already chosen. It now skips characters whose voice you locked or tuned, and leaves them exactly as you set them. Linking a character to one from an earlier book by hand now does the same (the link is not recorded on such a character, so it keeps its own voice), and declining a match no longer unlocks a voice you locked or tuned.
- **A character that already picked up an earlier book's voice keeps it until you change it.** In a series book analysed before this update, a locked or tuned character could have been given the voice of a same-named character from an earlier book. Castwright can't tell those mix-ups from links you wanted, so it leaves them. To choose a voice yourself, open the profile and pick one under "Or use a voice from My voices", then keep it locked. Choosing "Don't reuse" and then redesigning also gives the character its own voice. Assigning a library voice from the cast list keeps the earlier book's voice attached.
- **Re-analysing a book no longer swaps a voice you locked or tuned for one from an earlier book in the series.** When Castwright re-analysed a book that belongs to a series, it could match a character to one from an earlier book and hand the character that book's voice, even a voice you had locked or tuned. A locked or tuned character with a voice assigned now keeps it. "Start fresh" keeps that voice too, and only clears the link to the earlier book.
- **Re-analysing a book no longer splits a locked character in two.** If a locked character's name in Castwright's records changed between analyses, and an earlier book in the series had a character with the same name, re-analysis could leave you with two rows: the locked one with its voice but no lines, and a new one with the lines and a default voice. The character now stays one row, with its locked voice and its lines. When two rows with the same name do merge, a "not the same person" choice you made on either of them is kept, so a link you refused doesn't come back.
- **Adding or removing a character while Castwright is matching voices no longer leaves your cast unmatched.** The match now starts again with the cast you have, instead of quietly giving up.
- **When a voice can't be fully erased, Castwright no longer shows where your files live on disk.** If revoking consent or deleting a cloned voice leaves something behind, the reply a device on your network receives no longer reveals where your files sit on this computer.
- **Going back and forth to the cast screen while Castwright isn't answering no longer piles up stuck requests.** A screen that is waiting on your book now stops waiting when you leave it, and keeps trying again, a little more patiently each time, until your book arrives. The same goes for Restructure: if Castwright stops answering while it re-reads your book after a merge or split, your edits are no longer locked until you reload, and the chapter list shows the new chapters instead of the ones you just merged. If the fresh copy of the book hasn't loaded yet, edits stay paused until it has, so nothing you change in that gap can overwrite the new chapter numbering.
- **Re-parsing a book twice before re-analysing it no longer forgets the voices you designed.** The same goes for re-parsing and then replacing the manuscript: the voices you designed are still there when you analyse the book again.

# Castwright 1.14.0

- **The confusing GPU-memory settings are gone — Castwright is learning to manage your graphics card by itself.** The old Advanced Settings for GPU memory (a budget number, per-engine "weights", a coexist threshold) were a fiddly guessing game that changed depending on which card you had plugged in. They're now retired in favour of groundwork that reads your card's *actual* free memory and reserves exactly what each voice needs before it runs — not just before it speaks, but before loading a voice model or designing a new voice too — so switching between your laptop card and an external GPU needs no settings change, and two jobs can't quietly collide and run out of memory. After validation on real single- and dual-GPU hardware, this automatic placement is now **on by default** — it reserves what each voice needs before it runs and steers work to whichever card has room; a single "reserve" cushion is the only knob left, and you can switch the whole thing off if you'd rather run one job at a time. On a two-card machine it now frees up room on the card that actually needs it, leaving an idle voice on your *other* card undisturbed.
- **Castwright now supports Chinese and Japanese books.** Any book, performed by a full cast, now stretches to two of the world's most-spoken languages — upload a Chinese or Japanese manuscript and it's no longer turned away at the door. For the cleanest read on who's speaking, we recommend picking a local Qwen analysis model in Advanced Settings for these languages for now.
- **The Android app now stretches out on a tablet.** On a tablet or an unfolded foldable, Castwright's companion app shows your library and the player side by side, instead of one blown-up phone screen — tap a book on the left and it starts playing on the right, switch between books without losing your place, and turn the device however you like; your audiobook keeps playing right through the rotation. Bigger cover art fills the extra room — with comfortable margins so covers never run to the very edge of the screen — and on a phone everything looks exactly as it did before.

- **Analysis now runs on your own machine by default — and never quietly goes to the cloud without telling you.** Castwright is local-first, so a fresh install reads your book and works out who's speaking right on your own computer with Ollama; the free Gemini API is still one click away in setup if you'd rather use the cloud. Reviewing the script now runs on that same local model too — it used to ignore your choice and try a cloud model anyway, which on some setups could sit stuck on "Waiting for model" indefinitely without ever starting. And if you'd added a Gemini key just for voices, it's no longer secretly used for analysis: cloud fallback stays **on by default** (so nothing changes for your existing setup), but it's now a plain switch in analyzer settings you can turn **off** to keep analysis strictly on your machine — and whenever Castwright does fall back to Gemini, it says so, out loud, instead of switching behind your back. If your local model is still warming up, Castwright now waits for it patiently instead of giving up after a few seconds and calling it "unreachable." And when a chapter genuinely can't be reviewed, you'll see it plainly with a one-tap **Retry**, instead of the progress bar quietly emptying as if nothing happened.
- **Script review now shows a live heartbeat — which model is working, and how far through the chapter — and recovers cleanly when your local model is warming up.** The progress pill ticks through clear states: "Analyzing", "Loading model", "Reading", and "Streaming", so you see exactly what's happening. Large chapters no longer freeze at 0% while analysis runs behind the scenes. When the review finds a character who isn't in your cast yet, you name them once — every line they speak comes along — instead of being asked to create the same person over and over, line after line. Castwright now sizes every cloud request to stay under the limit, splitting an over-large chapter into pieces so it goes through, correctly waits out a "too many requests, try again shortly" response instead of mistaking it for a hard daily cap, and can no longer hang on a request that's too big. Analysis on your own machine benefits from the same splitting, so a very long chapter no longer gets truncated part-way.

It's off by a gentle default and lives in Advanced Settings ("Ollama analyzer concurrency"): leave it as-is and nothing changes, or nudge it up if you've got a capable card and a stack of books to get through. Castwright keeps it safe automatically — turning it up never squeezes your voices' performance engine, and on a smaller card it won't try to hold two different models at once.

Now the analyzer is required to decide each character's gender every time, so your cast comes back properly labelled and the merge step can do its job — keeping women and men in separate rows the way it was always meant to. If you already have a book with blank genders, re-analysing it (or setting a couple by hand) is all it takes.
