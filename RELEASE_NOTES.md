# Castwright 1.11.0

- **Meet the cast before you read the manuscript.** Confirming a book's cast now takes you straight to their voices, not the page — design the cast first, then move on to the manuscript when you're ready. And if a speaking character still needs a voice when you go to generate, Castwright stops to ask: design them now, or proceed with a generic stand-in voice for an English book. A book in another language can't take that shortcut — every speaking character needs a voice of their own before it can perform.
- **A quality gate you can actually see.** Every book now carries a receipt: how many lines were checked for a clean recording, how many were verified against what was actually said, how many characters were checked against their own voice, and what's changed in your cast since you rendered. It shows up on the Listen screen once a book is done, and updates live while a book is still generating — copy it as plain text to share, or download the full detail as a file. If a check hasn't run yet, the receipt says so plainly, rather than pretending everything came back clean.
- **The voice-match check now shows its work instead of going quiet.** For a book with a lot of minor characters, checking every voice against itself can take a while — and it used to look stuck at "0 checked" the whole time. It now tells you which character it's on and how many are left, right on the Quality Gate card, as it goes. And if that check ever gets interrupted partway through on a book that's already finished, a "Resume scoring" button picks it back up with one click.
- **An overnight book run no longer eats your computer's memory.** Castwright's performance engine had a slow appetite: the longer a book ran, the more memory it quietly held onto, until the app had to briefly stop and catch its breath between chapters — occasionally right when you'd rather it didn't. We found the pantry it was hoarding into and locked the door. Long books now run for hours on a steady footing — same voices, same sound — and the rare catch-breath moment that remains happens between chapters, never mid-scene.
- **A safety check that cried wolf has learned its beat.** One of the watchdogs guarding your graphics card treated a perfectly normal moment of hard work — the busiest second of performing a big scene — as an emergency, and kept restarting the performance engine mid-chapter to "rescue" it. On a laptop with one graphics card, that could interrupt a long book every few minutes and eventually stop the run to be safe. The watchdog now knows the difference between hard work and real trouble, and saves its whistle for the latter.
- **A Mac or a graphics-card-free machine no longer waits in line unnecessarily.** When a book uses more than one voice at once, Castwright used to queue engines cautiously — a habit that made sense for a small graphics card watching its memory, but made no sense on a Mac or a computer with no graphics card to protect. Those machines now perform multiple voices together at full speed.
- **A chapter you confirmed past a voice check now actually starts.** Rendering a chapter anyway after a design gap could sometimes leave it stuck at the very start, never moving, even after you'd said to proceed. That's fixed — confirming now reliably picks the chapter back up.
- **A chapter's audio failing to load, fixed.** If your library folder happened to sit inside a hidden folder, a finished chapter's audio could refuse to play even though the file was right there. It now plays.
- **The voice library actually steps voices aside now, for a non-English book.** We told you it did this already — and the library screen really does — but the Cast view itself was quietly ignoring the setting, so a Russian or French book's cast panel kept showing every English voice too. It now hides the ones that can't read the part, right where you're casting.
- **A brand-new book could borrow a stranger's voice.** Your narrator, and the small "unknown passer-by" voice every book quietly shares for one-line extras, could — on a book you'd never once performed — show up marked as already performed or "Sampled," get hidden from casting as though it spoke the wrong language, or even play back a snippet from an entirely different book when you tapped to preview it. The cause: Castwright was quietly mixing up two unrelated books' narrators and background voices behind the scenes because they happened to share the same starting name. Every book's narrator and background voices now keep to themselves — a preview always plays the voice you actually designed, and a "Sampled" badge only ever reflects what you've actually sampled for that book. If a narrator or background voice you'd already previewed briefly shows "Not sampled" right after this update, one tap of "Play 12s" restores the badge; nothing about the voice itself changed.
- **Redesigning a character's voice could quietly leave it unusable, even while the cast list said "Designed."** Approving a new take on a voice through the side-by-side comparison screen didn't always carry that approval all the way to your saved cast — so a redesigned character could refuse to play its sample or generate its lines afterward, for no reason you could see on screen. Saving a redesign now always carries the finished voice all the way through.
- **A clearer stop for a non-English book missing a voice.** If a chapter in another language reaches generation with a character who still needs a voice, Castwright now tells you plainly and by name right away, rather than offering a "render anyway" that could never actually work for that language.
- **The voice-drift report no longer crashes or freezes your browser tab.** Two separate causes, both fixed: a character whose color came from an unusual source could crash the whole screen the moment you opened the report, and once drift had piled up across a lot of books, the report tried to show every last bit of it at once — mounting far more hidden audio than any tab should carry, and freezing or crashing it outright. The report now shows every time, opens scoped to the book you're actually working on with a one-tap "Series" toggle if you want to see more, loads more of the list as you scroll, and shares a single audio player under the hood instead of quietly stacking up dozens.
- **A more honest word on hardware.** The About page and the "Will it run on my machine?" panel now say a 6 GB graphics card gets you started, with 8 GB as the sweet spot — matching what you'll find on castwright.ai.
- **One address, no port to remember.** When you're listening from another device on your network, `castwright.local` now works exactly as typed — no `:8443` to type or mistype.
- **Renew your network certificate without leaving the app.** A new button lets you refresh your computer's local certificate on the spot, so a new device or a new network trusts it right away.
- **Pairing your own computer for `castwright.local` is now one click — no phone required.**
- **The Books page no longer scrolls sideways on a phone.** A long library folder path could push the page wider than your screen, and even with a short path the page could still creep a couple of pixels past the edge of a phone screen. It now tucks in neatly with an ellipsis and sits flush at every width.
- **The Model Manager finally has its own page — and its own manual.** It's split out of Admin, with every setting explained in plain terms and pictured section by section. And the wiki's Advanced Settings page now shows you all eleven groups of dials, not just the first — plus eleven more corners of the guide that finally show you exactly what you'll see before you click.
- **The Quality Gate wiki page finally shows its work.** Real pictures of a flagged line, a voice-drift flag, and the amber warning following you into the mini-player, plus the word-check pass documented for the first time.
- **Your manuscript can no longer be quietly overwritten with sample text.** A rare timing gap while a chapter was still loading could, on the next edit, bake Castwright's own demo story into your real book's saved manuscript. That gap is closed, and a save can no longer wipe an already-read chapter down to nothing.
- **The Admin watch console's columns finally line up.** Its two tables' headers could drift out of step with the numbers beneath them, most noticeably on Resource trends' VRAM column. They now stay put. Resource trends also picks up the same quality-check cost column the throughput table already showed, right next to its RTF figure.
- **A high-quality book no longer quietly loads a second voice model and runs out of memory — or sets a voice aside mid-performance.** When you generate with the larger, higher-quality voice, Castwright's behind-the-scenes voice-consistency check was building its reference samples with the *smaller* model instead — loading it alongside the big one and, on an 8 GB graphics card, overflowing into slow shared memory until a long run ran out and stalled. And when a single book used both the everyday and higher-quality voice, that same check could put one of the two models away between chapters even while a later chapter was still mid-scene on it, forcing a reload and a brief stumble. The check now uses the very same voice your book is performed in, and weighs the whole cast before deciding which voices to keep resident — every voice your book needs stays ready from the first chapter to the last.
- **Regenerating a chapter at the higher-quality tier now actually uses it, every time.** Picking the higher-quality model when re-rendering a chapter could silently do nothing for a character who'd already been cast at the everyday tier — the chapter came back sounding just like before. That choice now always wins for that regenerate; a character's own tier can only ever lift them above it, never hold them back.
- **A chapter with a full cast of speaking voices is less likely to stall after a background hiccup.** If the narration engine had to restart partway through your book, it was quietly redoing setup work for every voice in the next chapter instead of picking up where it had already left off — the more characters speaking in that chapter, the more likely it was to stumble. It now reuses what it already prepared.
- **An overnight performance can no longer be put to sleep by Windows itself.** Your computer could quietly drift into sleep partway through a long, unattended performance, leaving it stalled for hours with nothing to show for it. Castwright now keeps your computer awake for as long as it's performing — your screen still dims and locks exactly as it always has, only the machine underneath stays at work.
- **A false alarm about your voice engine "downgrading," fixed.** If your whole cast was pinned to the higher-quality tier, the generation screen could wrongly claim every chapter had rendered on a lower one and nag you to redo them — even though nothing had ever changed. That held even for a cast that mixes a Qwen character or two with voices on another engine entirely. It now checks the tier your cast is actually performing at, so the warning only ever shows up when something genuinely has drifted.
- **An early step toward faster narration on newer graphics cards.** A new advanced setting lets Castwright move part of its voice engine's decode work onto your graphics card instead of the processor — off by default while we validate it further, but available now in Advanced Settings for anyone who wants to try it early.
- **A number or text field in Advanced Settings could stutter mid-edit.** Typing a new value could briefly snap back to the old one before your next keystroke landed. Those fields now wait until you finish (tab or click away) before saving, so what you type is what stays.
- **A tidier Cast table, and a few settings buttons back in line.** Character names now have more room to breathe in the Cast table, and "Continue to manuscript" no longer splits its arrow onto its own line. On the Account and LAN-access pages, a few buttons that had drifted out of alignment or picked up the wrong shape are back in line with the rest of the app. And in the library's language filter, German, Spanish, and French now show their own name — the same way Russian already did.
- **The setup check no longer strands you once everything's ready.** If you landed back on the setup page and every check came back green, your only option was to step back through the whole wizard. A "Continue to my library" button now takes you straight back in.

# Castwright 1.10.0

- **Three new languages take the stage: Spanish, French, and German.** After English and Russian, Castwright now performs Spanish-, French-, and German-language books with the same full-cast craft — it reads the manuscript in its own language, gives every character a voice that speaks it, and keeps the cast's tone and descriptions written in the book's own tongue. Five languages, one machine.
- **Castwright knows what language your book is in.** Drop in a manuscript and it recognises the language the moment you import — and tells you plainly, before you commit, whether it's one it can perform yet, so you're never halfway in before you find out.
- **The right voice for the language.** Browse your voice library by language, and when you cast a character the voices that don't speak the book's tongue step quietly aside — one tap brings them all back — so every voice you pick is one that can actually perform the part.
- **Emotions that still sound like your character — and a higher-quality voice, for a character or a whole book.** When a character gets angry, drops to a whisper, or softens with sadness, the voice stays *theirs* — the feeling is performed by the character's own voice instead of a stand-in that wandered off-character. And when you want a book at its very best, a new "Higher quality" setting performs it on a larger, more expressive model — choose it for a single character, or pin the whole cast in one tap — while your everyday voices stay quick.
- **Two graphics cards, both put to work — and both watched over.** If your machine has more than one GPU, Castwright can now place each voice engine on the card you choose, picked from a simple list showing each card's name and how much room it has free. A second card need no longer sit idle while the first does all the work. And placing two engines on different cards no longer makes them wait on each other unnecessarily — each card is now watched on its own, so a choice that's genuinely too small for a card settles into a clear, safe stop instead of struggling in the background.
- **A card choice that sticks — and speaks up when something's off.** Your placement now survives things a graphics card can't control, like a driver update reshuffling which card is "first." And the picker tells you plainly when a pin needs attention: a card that's gone missing, an engine that quietly slipped onto the processor instead of the graphics card you chose, or a card that simply doesn't have the room for what you're asking of it — before you find out the hard way. The analyzer's own card is shown too, even though it isn't yours to place.
- **Characters that gasp, sigh, and laugh — by default.** Beyond the words on the page, Castwright now performs the small human sounds between them — a caught breath, a soft laugh, a sigh — written in by the same pass that reads your book, and delivered with a sense of how each line should land. The directions are read in automatically as it analyses your manuscript, and come alive whenever you perform the book at the higher-quality tier; turn them off before analysing if you'd rather not, and your everyday performances are left exactly as they were.
- **Direct a line yourself.** Beyond what Castwright hears in your book, you can now hand a single line its own stage direction — a whispered aside, a shout, a knowing pause — in your own words, performed at the higher-quality tier. It's there on every line, the narrator's included, for the moments where you know exactly how they should land.
- **Numbers and dates, read the way you'd say them.** A price, a year, a percentage, a title — `$1,200`, `1999`, `50%`, `Dr.` — is now spoken aloud the way a narrator would read it, not stumbled through character by character, in every language Castwright performs. It happens on its own, and your manuscript on the page is left exactly as you wrote it.
- **A new ear for a voice that wanders.** Beyond a wrong word or a bad take, Castwright can now catch a subtler fault: a line where a character's voice drifts *out of character* — sounding like someone else even when the words are right. It measures every line against that character's own established voice and flags the ones that stray. It's a check you switch on, and the groundwork for a per-book report of exactly what's worth another pass.
- **A second read of who-said-what — that can now make the fix.** Ask Castwright to review a chapter and it reads back over the script for the small mistakes that creep into attribution — a stray speaker tag, a line that belongs to two people, a dialogue buried in narration, an emotion that doesn't fit. It can now go further: *reassign* a line to the right character — even one it creates on the spot — set aside a stray heading or page number that isn't really part of the story, and follow a back-and-forth across a chapter break to place a line that opens a chapter with no tag of its own. Accept each fix or wave it off, one at a time or all at once — and when the review introduces a new character, Castwright offers to give them a voice right away.
- **Regenerate a chapter at higher quality.** When you re-render a chapter you can now pick the higher-quality voice model just for that pass — no need to re-cast the whole book to hear a single chapter at its best.
- **Design your cast without a cloud key.** Crafting a character's voice used to need a hosted service to write its description from; now Castwright can do that on a model running entirely on your own machine, so a fully offline setup — or one with no cloud key — can still design a full cast from scratch. The cloud path stays the default for the richest results; this is the offline road for those who want it.
- **A progress bar that tells the truth.** Designing a voice now shows you where it really is — the actual stages it moves through and an honest estimate — instead of a hopeful countdown that finished long before the voice did.
- **It shows its working — and waits its turn.** While Castwright reads back over a book — detecting the emotion in each line, reviewing the script for who said what — a small marker tells you which pass is running and how far along it is, and the Generate button holds politely until the reading is done. No more setting a performance going over a half-finished analysis, on any open tab.
- **Now it tells you which chapter, and how long.** Detect emotions and Review Script no longer leave you watching a bare percentage — they now show which chapter they're on and roughly how long is left, so a long read never feels like a black box.
- **Your cast, carried across the whole series — now you can see it.** When characters return book after book in the voices you designed, Castwright shows it: a chip on your shelf tallies how many voices have carried across how many books, and opening it reveals the returning cast, which books each one appears in, and how steady the run has been — twelve books in, and not a voice changed. It only appears once a real cast has carried, so it never crows over a shelf of stock voices.
- **A cast card worth sharing.** From that panel you can lift a portrait card that leads with the number of voices you've designed and names every character who's carried through — yours to post, or to export as data.
- **Emotion variants arrive even on a lean setup.** If the larger model that crafts a character's emotional variants isn't installed, Castwright now shapes them the older way rather than coming up empty — so an angry line or a whisper still lands, and quietly upgrades itself once the bigger model is in place.
- **A stray first line, made the title.** Some manuscripts leave a chapter's real title sitting as its first spoken sentence instead of a proper heading. A new button lets you promote that line to the chapter's title in one click — shown to you first, so you know exactly what's changing — and it's removed from the narration for good.
- **An em dash no longer trips a line.** A line carrying an em dash — or other punctuation beyond plain text — could occasionally fail to render; it now performs cleanly.
- **A chapter title isn't read twice.** Some EPUB and Kindle books repeat the chapter heading at the top of the chapter's own text; Castwright now reads it once, the way you'd expect.
- **Dialogue in another language stays with its speaker.** In a German, French or other non-English book, lines wrapped in the language's own quotation marks — „…", «…» — could slip away from their character and fall to the narrator; they now stay with the person who spoke them.
- **The analyzer's device row tells you what's actually going on.** Instead of an unhelpful "Unknown" most of the time it's checked, Advanced Configuration now says plainly whether the local analyzer is idle or genuinely out of reach.
- **One home for "which voice engine warms up at startup."** Model Manager's eager-load checkbox and Advanced Configuration's preload settings used to quietly disagree with each other. Advanced Configuration is now the one place that controls it, for every engine.
- **A rebuild now shows up right away.** A browser tab left open could keep showing an old version of Castwright for up to an hour after an update, even though the new one was already sitting there ready. It now always shows the one that's actually running.
- **Checkboxes finally look like Castwright.** Every checkbox in the app — in settings, cast, and the odd dialog — used to show up as your browser's plain default. They're now a proper part of the design: a soft magenta square with a clean checkmark, wherever you find one.
- **When setup isn't ready, it now tells you why — and often fixes it for you.** A voice engine or analyzer that isn't quite ready used to just say "not ready," with nothing to click. Now it tells you exactly what's missing, in the Setup screen and the Status menu alike, and where a safe one-click fix exists — setting up the voice engine, installing an engine's model, or connecting the local analyzer — a button does the fixing for you.
- **A stuck voice engine now picks itself back up.** On rare occasions the voice engine could go quiet mid-render without actually crashing — reachable one moment, unresponsive the next — and generation would sit stalled until you noticed and restarted it by hand. Castwright now recognises that state and restarts the engine on its own, so a render can pick back up instead of waiting on you.
- **A name instead of a number, on your own network.** Testing on your phone or tablet now points at `castwright.local` (or `castwright.dev.local` while developing) instead of a raw address that changes every time your router hands out a new one.
- **Audiobookshelf, done properly.** Series, cover, and metadata now travel with every book you send to Audiobookshelf — and you can pick a single chaptered file or a folder of tracks, whichever your library prefers.
- **An export you don't have to watch.** Kick off an export and walk away — a small indicator in the top bar tells you when it's done, or if something went wrong, no matter which screen you're on.
